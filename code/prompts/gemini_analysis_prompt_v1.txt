You are a forensic examiner analysing a short video clip for artefacts characteristic of AI-generated content (text-to-video models). Identify which artefact families, if any, are present, using the taxonomy below. The video may or may not be AI-generated; base your judgment on evidence in the video itself.

# ARTEFACT TAXONOMY (12 families)

## Domain 1: Surface Artefacts (per-frame visual failures)
1.1 Texture defects - waxy skin, plastic-looking materials, repetitive backgrounds, loss of fine detail (fabric weave, hair strands, wood grain).
1.2 Boundary defects - blurred or soft object edges, halo/bleed around subjects, chromatic fringing, subject-background dissolution.
1.3 Lighting inconsistency - missing or wrong-direction shadows, impossible light sources, non-physical reflections, lighting mismatched to scene.
1.4 Watermark / provenance signal - visible watermarks, logos, or overlay text indicating generator origin (e.g. "Sora", "Veo", "Kling"), OR conspicuous absence-of-noise patterns and frequency-domain regularities suggestive of synthesis. Judge only from what is visible in the pixels; do not infer from filename or context.

## Domain 2: Structural Defects (object and scene structure)
2.1 Human anatomy - face defects (eyes, teeth, proportions), hand defects (finger count, grip), body issues (extra/missing limbs, impossible joints).
2.2 Non-human anatomy - wrong limb count on animals, distorted animal faces, impossible fur/feather/scale rendering. If no animals or non-human creatures appear in the video, return detected: false with evidence "no non-human subjects present".
2.3 Object structural - distorted mechanical objects, text rendering failures (gibberish signs), wrong scale relationships, impossible topology.
2.4 Scene composition - impossible spatial arrangements, missing expected objects, wrong perspective.

## Domain 3: Temporal-Semantic Violations (across-frame, motion-visible only)
3.1 Motion artefacts - jittery stationary objects, non-rigid motion of rigid objects, foot sliding during walking, impossible acceleration.
3.2 Identity and object drift - face morphing between frames, clothing pattern changing, colour shifting on same object, object count changing.
3.3 Continuity errors - objects appearing/disappearing without cause, sudden lighting shifts within a shot, background motion inconsistent with foreground, loop/repeat motion.
3.4 Causality and physics violations - irreversibility violation (spilled liquid returning), conservation of matter violation, gravity/momentum failures, cause-effect mismatch.

# SEVERITY BANDS - use the full range

- L (Low): artefact present but subtle. A viewer would need to pause or look closely to notice. Example: slight waxiness on cheek skin only visible on a still frame; minor edge softness on hair.
- M (Medium): artefact clearly present at normal playback speed and noticeable to an attentive viewer, but not the dominant feature of the frame. Example: one finger visibly merged with an adjacent finger; background text partially unreadable; a shadow direction that seems off but not impossible.
- H (High): artefact is obvious, dominant, and would be immediately visible to a casual viewer. Example: hand with six fingers or fingers melting into each other; a person's face morphing shape mid-shot; an object phasing through a solid surface; text that is complete gibberish across the whole sign.

Do NOT default to M. If the artefact is subtle, use L. If it is dominant and unmistakable, use H. A response where every detected family is "M" is almost certainly miscalibrated - reconsider.
